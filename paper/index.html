Structured Content Performance Archetypes

A reproducible workflow that turns large-scale search-performance measurements into descriptive content archetypes and a transparent, human-reviewed prioritization queue — not an automated content-editing system.

01

Executive summary

Problem. A large content inventory has no reliable, transparent signal for which pages deserve a reviewer's attention first. Manual review of every page does not scale.

Approach. March 2026 Google Search Console measurements were aggregated to the client-content level from the FlyRank ML Internship warehouse, represented with five performance features, and analyzed two ways: a transparent CTR opportunity baseline, and an unsupervised K-Means clustering of performance patterns, validated on clients the model had not seen during development.

What the model found. Three descriptive performance archetypes emerged consistently across development and held-out clients, and a transparent baseline identified 1,368 pages with a measurable CTR opportunity worth reviewing first.

What the output is for. The ranked queue and archetypes are decision support — a starting point for a human reviewer to investigate, not a verdict. Analysis is not the same as recommendation, and recommendation is not the same as action — each step below requires the next.

March GSC observations were aggregated to the client-content level. Impressions and clicks were log-transformed for their right-skewed distributions; missing values were median-imputed and features were standardized. K-Means was used as an unsupervised, descriptive method — candidate K from 2–8 were inspected, and K=3 was chosen for balancing silhouette performance, cluster size, stability, and interpretability rather than maximizing one metric alone.

Final clustering features

  • Impressions
  • Clicks
  • CTR
  • Average position
  • Position volatility

Excluded from the feature space

  • Identifiers (used only as keys, never as predictors)
  • Future measurements and trend-derived fields
  • Downstream baseline / action scores
  • Availability flags and unrelated GA4, traffic-source, and AI-referral fields

The transparent baseline flags pages with at least 500 impressions and CTR below the median for their average-position tier; its review score is log(1 + impressions) × max(expected CTR − observed CTR, 0).

05

Opportunity ranking

Decision support, not automatic content modification.

The transparent CTR opportunity baseline compares each page's observed CTR against the median CTR for pages at a similar average-position tier. Pages with enough visibility and a below-expected CTR are surfaced for review, ranked by opportunity score.

Priority code high_visibility_low_ctr — pages with meaningful visibility whose CTR under-performs their position tier are reviewed first, since they represent the clearest, most measurable opportunity.

Human review required. No automatic content changes are made. Every ranked page still needs a reviewer to confirm intent, SERP context, technical status, and business value before any action.

Recommendation workflow

Rank → Investigate → Validate → Act — each step is a gate, not a formality.

07

Limitations

  • The clusters are descriptive groupings, not ground-truth content categories or quality labels.
  • The analysis does not establish causality between observed metrics and future outcomes.
  • Search intent and underlying private queries are not directly observed.
  • SERP features, competitor context, indexing, canonicalization, redirects, and other technical conditions are not represented in the five clustering features.
  • Business value is not represented, so model priority is not the same as business priority.
  • The main feature vector is based on March 2026, so seasonality and longer-term content history are simplified.
  • Uneven GSC availability means the findings describe the measurable, GSC-covered portion of the inventory.
  • K-Means imposes a fixed three-group structure even though real performance patterns may overlap continuously.
  • Grouped validation tests structural consistency for unseen clients; it does not prove future performance.

Bottom line: this is a reproducible decision-support workflow, not a prediction of search-engine behaviour.

09

Final takeaway

ML output becomes useful when it can be inspected, challenged, and translated into a bounded human workflow. Here, the model organizes observed search-performance patterns — it does not replace the person making the content decision.

This analysis provides
  • Three validated, descriptive performance archetypes
  • A transparent, reproducible opportunity baseline
  • A ranked, human-reviewable queue of 1,368 pages
This analysis does not claim
  • Causality between metrics and future performance
  • Prediction of future search-engine behaviour
  • Authority to make automatic content changes
