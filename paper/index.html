01 — Question

Research question: What performance archetypes exist across the content inventory, and how can those observed patterns support content-review prioritization?

Abstract

This research asks what measurable performance archetypes exist across a large content inventory and how those patterns can support content-review decisions. Using the FlyRank internship warehouse, the analysis aggregates March 2026 Google Search Console measurements at the client-content level and represents each page using impressions, clicks, CTR, average position, and position volatility. A transparent CTR opportunity baseline is compared with an unsupervised K-Means clustering approach, with client-grouped validation and explicit leakage checks used to assess whether the observed structure is stable and whether the evaluation is honest. The analysis produced three descriptive performance clusters and a ranked review queue of 1,368 pages with positive baseline opportunity scores, with high-visibility, lower-CTR pages receiving the highest review priority. The resulting playbook is intended as decision support for human review rather than as an automated system for rewriting, pruning, merging, or predicting future search performance.

Decision supported: prioritize which pages deserve human investigation first, while keeping the final content or technical decision with a reviewer.

The analysis uses the FlyRank ML Internship warehouse, specifically the fact_content_daily_performance daily fact table. The verified warehouse scan contains 78,835,655 rows covering 2025-01-27 through 2026-06-30.

The analytical grain is report_date × client_hash_id × content_hash_id. March 2026 is the fixed development window. Daily GSC observations from that month are aggregated to the client-content level, producing 176,738 feature vectors.

Features

Excluded

Identifiers were not used as numeric predictors. Future measurements, trend-derived fields, downstream baseline scores, availability flags, and unrelated GA4/traffic-source/AI-referral fields were excluded from the clustering feature space.

Client names, URLs, private queries, and other identifying information are not published in this artifact.

03 — Methodology

Feature construction

March GSC observations were aggregated to the client-content level. Impressions and clicks were log-transformed because their distributions were strongly right-skewed. Missing feature values were median-imputed and the resulting features were standardized.

Transparent baseline

The baseline identifies pages with at least 500 impressions whose observed CTR is below the median CTR for their average-position tier. The review score is log(1 + impressions) × max(expected CTR − observed CTR, 0).

K-Means clustering

Because there is no ground-truth archetype label, K-Means was used as an unsupervised descriptive method. Candidate values of K from 2 through 8 were inspected. K=3 was selected because it balanced silhouette performance, cluster size, stability, and interpretability rather than simply maximizing one metric.

Validation

The primary validation used a client-grouped 80/20 development/holdout design. Clients were kept entirely within either development or holdout, avoiding client overlap between the two sets. A random row split was also examined as a comparison but was not treated as the primary unseen-client test.

Leakage audit

The final feature set uses March measurements only. April 2026 measurements were used only for a separate leakage check and excluded from the final feature vector. Identifiers, future measurements, trend-derived labels, and downstream action scores were excluded. Preprocessing was fitted within the development split and applied to holdout data.

05 — Limitations & honest framing

  • The clusters are descriptive groupings, not ground-truth content categories or quality labels.
  • The analysis does not establish causality between observed metrics and future outcomes.
  • Search intent and underlying private queries are not directly observed.
  • SERP features, competitor context, indexing, canonicalization, redirects, and other technical conditions are not represented in the five clustering features.
  • Business value is not represented, so model priority is not the same as business priority.
  • The main feature vector is based on March 2026, so seasonality and longer-term content history are simplified.
  • Uneven GSC availability means the findings describe the measurable GSC-covered portion of the inventory.
  • K-Means imposes a fixed three-group structure even though real performance patterns may overlap continuously.
  • Grouped validation tests structural consistency for unseen clients; it does not prove future performance.

Bottom line: this is a reproducible decision-support workflow, not a prediction of search-engine behaviour.

06 — Ranked recommendations

Rank → Investigate → Validate → Act

1. High-visibility CTR review

Reason code: high_visibility_low_ctr

Start with pages that have meaningful observed visibility and a positive CTR opportunity score. Review search intent, SERP presentation, title/meta alignment, competing results, and page relevance before any change.

2. Low-visibility, relatively well-ranked pages

Investigate search demand, query coverage, indexing, relevance, and whether the observed position is supported by enough impressions. A good average position alone is not evidence of a hidden opportunity.

3. Low-visibility, unstable pages

Investigate technical status, search demand, historical changes, content relevance, and data sufficiency before deciding on an action.

Human-review boundary

No automated rewriting, pruning, merging, canonical changes, quality classification, search-intent inference, or promise of ranking/traffic improvement is supported by this analysis.
